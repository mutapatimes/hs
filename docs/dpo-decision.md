# Data protection officer: decision under Article 37 (for signature)

Decision: Halia does not appoint a statutory data protection officer at this time. Privacy
responsibility sits with the founder, with external data protection counsel on retainer for review.
Contact for data protection matters: privacy@haliascore.com. Review this decision when any trigger
below changes, and at least annually.

## The three triggers in Article 37(1)

| Trigger | Applies? | Reasoning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| (a) Public authority or body | No | Halia is a private company |
| (b) Core activities consist of processing which, by its nature, scope or purposes, requires regular and systematic monitoring of data subjects on a large scale | Not at present | Halia's core activity is scoring merchants' customers on their behalf. The scoring is regular and systematic. It is not at present large scale: Halia serves a small number of merchants with client books in the low thousands, processes each book in memory for minutes, and retains nothing. The ICO's guidance treats scale by reference to the number of data subjects, volume, duration and geographic extent; on each measure Halia is currently small. This trigger is the one most likely to change as merchants are added |
| (c) Core activities consist of large-scale processing of special category or criminal-offence data | No | Halia processes neither; origin-proxy signals are off by default |

## What Halia does instead

- A named privacy lead (the founder) with authority over product decisions affecting personal data.
- External counsel engaged for the review of the compliance pack and for advice on request.
- The record of processing, DPIA support, legitimate interests assessment and incident response
  procedure maintained in the repository under docs/.
- Data protection by design as an engineering rule: zero retention, offline reference data, origin
  proxies off by default, consent surfaced before messaging.

## When to revisit

Appoint a DPO, or reassess, when any of the following occurs: more than roughly 20 active merchants
or 250,000 customer records scored per month; a merchant with a book above 100,000 customers; any
processing of special category data; expansion into a jurisdiction whose law imposes a lower
threshold (for example Germany's 20-person rule); or counsel's advice to the contrary.
